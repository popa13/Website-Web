10-22 Alejandro Morales Borrero UQAM What would a Mandelbrot set look like in three dimensions ?

Abstract : In the talk, we will discuss what enumerative combinatorics is. We will then turn to fantastic integer sequences and where to find them in the mathematical and computational universes. We will give an overview of, and some new perspectives on, well-known sequences such as the Fibonacci numbers, the Euler numbers, and the ubiquity of the Catalan numbers. Finally, we will talk about partial orders and the enumeration of the linear extensions of a few of them.
10-08 Pierre-Olivier Parisé UQTR What would a Mandelbrot set look like in three dimensions ?

Abstract : The Mandelbrot set is a fascinating object. Defined through the iteration of a polynomial of degree two, it captures many phenomena closely tied to the filled Julia set of a quadratic polynomial. Since the 1980s, many have attempted to generalize these sets to three-dimensional space using various techniques. Notable examples include Norton's quaternionic Julia sets and the triplex numbers of White and Nylander, which led to what is known as the Mandelbulb.

In this talk, I will give a brief overview of the theory of polynomial iteration and introduce our main protagonists: the Mandelbrot set and filled Julia sets. I will then describe some of these attempts to generalize the sets to three-dimensional space. I will also explain why a direct generalization to three dimensions might be impossible. Finally, I will present an approach that generalizes the Mandelbrot set and filled Julia sets using multicomplex numbers, as well as related work in this setting.

The last part of the talk, devoted to multicomplex filled Julia sets, is joint work with Quentin Charles.

09-24 Samuel Valiquette UdeS What does it depend on? An introduction to multivariate modelling

Abstract : In probability and statistics, it is often necessary to model and study the random behaviour of several random variables simultaneously. A fundamental question is then to quantify and describe the dependence relationships between the components of a random vector of arbitrary dimension. But what exactly do we mean by dependence? Given two distinct random vectors, how can we determine which one exhibits a stronger dependence structure than the other?

In this talk, we will try to answer these questions by introducing the concept of a copula. We will first motivate its definition through an example of dependence modelling, and then formalize it using Sklar's fundamental theorem (1959). We will then examine some important properties, as well as a particular class of copulas. Finally, we will see how copulas make it possible to quantify the degree of dependence between random variables through dependence measures that are expressed solely in terms of the copula, such as Kendall's tau.
